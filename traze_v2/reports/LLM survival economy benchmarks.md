# Traze's novelty lies in validity, not survival

**Bottom line.** Across the notes (compiled to 18 Nov 2026), no verified paper combines what Traze combines: a multi-agent compute economy, a rank-based tax/subsidy, a one-shot versus gradual elimination comparison, and a test of whether survival order recovers capability. The claim "LLM agents whose survival depends on their own token spend" is not novel. The Energy Society (arXiv 2607.14865) already charges agents energy for generated tokens and deactivates them at zero energy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14865)). USACOArena already bills agents in API-price-normalised credits plus an optional wall-clock term ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10182)). The rank-incentive "lemma" (payoff is monotone in effort iff transfers are non-increasing in rank number) is close to tautological, and its economic content has precedent in tax-notch bunching and in the sports-economics literature on "winning by losing". Token-versus-time metering has direct precedent, because USACOArena disables its time term for the headline results over latency unfairness. Hidden-reasoning billing is documented provider behaviour, though no benchmark I verified charges for it as currency. The defensible novelty is narrower: a planted-skill validity test of elimination and economy rankings, with the removal rule and the transfer schedule as experimental factors. That claim rests on a **negative search of about 6 to 15 queries per topic**, mostly reading abstracts and snippets. It must be worded "to our knowledge" and re-checked on Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar before submission.

**How to read this report.** Verification flags: FULL = full text or HTML body read (through a summarising fetcher, so numbers are as relayed); ABS = abstract or bibliographic record only; SNIP = search-result snippet only; MEM = recalled from memory, not verified in this work. Note that the fetcher was a summarising model, so even "FULL" items carry transcription risk. Many 2026 identifiers and model names (for example "Astra", "Opus 5.5", "gpt-5.5") come only from fetched pages and were not independently checked. Do not cite any arXiv ID below without opening it first.

## Survival economies exist, but none ties death to rank

The nearest prior art ties an agent's death to its own token spend, which is the thing most likely to be mistaken for Traze's core idea. **The Energy Society** (Hansen, Torrielli, Tonini, Galke Poech; arXiv 2607.14865, 16 Jul 2026, cs.MA, accepted at AITC 2026) makes agents spend energy on generated tokens (cost depends on model size), regain it through jobs or donations, and deactivate at zero. It compares competitive and cooperative objectives. Its qualitative findings: larger models burn the most energy and spend more than they earn, cooperative incentives produce costly donations to revive others, and sabotage is rare. Elimination is an absolute threshold (energy = 0), not a rank. I saw no capability-validity question in the abstract ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14865); ABS, plus a SNIP re code on GitHub at [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14865)).

The other survival-flavoured work uses death or scarcity for different ends. Masumori and Ikegami's Sugarscape study (arXiv 2508.12920, Aug 2025) kills agents at zero energy and reports attack rates above 80% under extreme scarcity in the strongest models, with compliance on a poison task falling from 100% to 33% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12920); ABS). Pasichnyk's "Yerkes-Dodson Curve for AI Agents" (arXiv 2603.07360, Mar 2026) varies upkeep cost across 22 grid-world experiments and finds trades peak at medium pressure (29 at upkeep 5, versus 8 to 12 at the extremes) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07360); ABS). It is the closest thing to a pressure-level sweep, but it measures cooperation, not ranking validity. Shutdown-threat papers ("Survive at All Costs", arXiv 2603.05028; Palisade's shutdown-resistance study, arXiv 2509.14260; "Survival Games", arXiv 2505.17937) study misbehaviour under threat, not ranking ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05028), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17937); ABS and SNIP). DECIDE-SIM (arXiv 2509.12190) and SQUID GAME (arXiv 2511.10691) were seen only as snippets and should not be cited without opening them.

Among economies and arenas, the closest structural analogues are Vending-Bench Arena (competition plus early termination), EnterpriseArena (survival plus tool-call cost) and USACOArena (own-compute billing); none combines all three. Vending-Bench (Backlund and Petersson, arXiv 2502.15840) scores end net worth, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet at a mean of $2,217.93 against a single human at $844.05 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840); FULL). EnterpriseArena (arXiv 2603.23638) ends an episode at zero score if cash goes negative, penalises 5,000 per tool call, and reports only about **15 to 16% of runs surviving**, with human experts at 60% survival ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23638); FULL; the 16% versus 15.4% discrepancy is unresolved). Market-Bench (arXiv 2604.05523) pits 20 LLM retailers in budget-limited auctions, with profit tracking fill rate (rho 0.88) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05523); FULL). CoffeeBench (arXiv 2606.16613) and EcoGym (arXiv 2602.09514) are multi-agent or long-horizon economies scored on money, with small seed counts (CoffeeBench: 3 seeds) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16613), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09514); FULL). None of these uses a leaderboard rank to set taxes, subsidies or removal.

On the specific question of removal rule or rank-based redistribution versus how well survival tracks capability, the notes found **nothing**. This is about 6 queries on survival and about 15 on economic benchmarks, so it is a limited negative. The nearest items are the budget-constrained agent-selection paper (arXiv 2611.21515, successive halving, SNIP only) and DarwinX (arXiv 2608.07545, SNIP only); neither is confirmed relevant ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21515), [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07545)).

## Rankings from games are reported with uncertainty but rarely checked against capability

Agent Island (Connacher Murphy, arXiv 2605.04312, May 2026) is the strongest comparator. Seven of 49 models play a Survivor-style game with private elimination votes. Skill is ranked with a Bayesian Plackett-Luce model (Gibbs sampler, 2,000 iterations, 500 burn-in) using only the winner, which the author says slows identification. Over 999 games gpt-5.5 leads (posterior mean 5.64, 95% CI 3.73 to 7.97), but ranks 2 and 3 (3.10 versus 2.86) are statistically indistinguishable, with the difference interval [-0.88, +1.35]. Voters favour a same-provider finalist by **+8.3 percentage points** (95% CI 4.7 to 11.8), which the author concedes violates the model's no-matchup-effect assumption ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04312v1); FULL via fetcher). In my reading of the notes it does not validate its ranking against an external capability measure; that absence claim comes from a summarised fetch and must be verified in the full text. Because elimination is by peer vote, it measures social skill and bias, not economic efficiency; this is a concrete example of a peer-voted elimination being a confounded capability signal.

Other ranking-method evidence supports the general worry. Chatbot Arena moved to batch Bradley-Terry ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chiang24b.html); SNIP). "Elo Uncovered" finds Elo order-sensitive and unreliable for similar-strength models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17295); SNIP). Non-transitivity makes fixed-baseline rankings baseline-dependent ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14074); SNIP). "The Leaderboard Illusion" documents selective private testing (one provider with 27 private variants) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879); SNIP). Construct-validity work reviews 445 benchmarks and finds only 16% use statistical tests when comparing models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703); SNIP). Sandbagging work shows models can strategically underperform on evaluations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07358v4); SNIP), which is the agent-side analogue of the incentive problem below. The notes found no paper that tests, with hidden ground-truth skill, whether an economy or game ranking rule recovers capability; existing validations use external proxy leaderboards (Xu et al. correlate with Chatbot Arena) or none. The supportable statement is that planted-skill recovery is a distinct validation design, with a limited search behind it.

The economy benchmarks also bear on whether outcomes track capability. Where budget was varied, capability, not budget, was the bottleneck. USACOArena shows a 10M-credit budget drops Gemini from 13.2 to 8.3 while 40M leaves it at 13.0 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10182); FULL). EnterpriseArena failures are reactive rather than budget-driven ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23638); FULL). Market-Bench finds Phi-4 14B matching GPT-4o on profit and beating ERNIE 4.5 300B ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05523); FULL). Scoring-target effects are shown by CoffeeBench: under a revenue target one model proposed circular trades at inflated prices, and one prompt sentence removed them ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16613); FULL; one evaluated model, one market). Epoch rates Vending-Bench 2 "Flawed" because a "use any tactic" prompt earned over $1M in 102 simulated days against about $63K for a good strategy ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review); FULL). Regimes where compute itself is the scarce currency, which is Traze's setting, are largely untested.

For one-shot versus gradual elimination, the evidence is theoretical and indirect. Successive halving (Jamieson and Talwalkar, arXiv 1502.07943; Hyperband, arXiv 1603.06560) and fixed-confidence elimination (Even-Dar et al. 2002; Karnin et al., ICML 2013) support discarding gradually as evidence accumulates ([arXiv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1502.07943), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/karnin13.html); SNIP). Braverman and Mossel (arXiv 0707.1051) show that even optimal aggregation of single noisy comparisons leaves constant average displacement, a useful reference bound for a one-shot cull ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1051); SNIP). Sziklai, Biro and Csato's Monte-Carlo study finds Swiss formats recover full rankings best ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.06023); SNIP). **No source quantifies survival-order rank correlation under one-shot versus gradual culls**, so the prediction is an inference; an empirical test of it is where Traze can claim novelty. For metrics, Harrell's C is biased upward under heavy censoring (about 49% in one simulation) and Uno's IPCW C corrects this under covariate-independent censoring ([scikit-survival docs](https://scikit-survival.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user_guide/evaluating-survival-models.html), [PMC3079915](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079915); SNIP). Survivors at the horizon in Traze are right-censored, and one-shot culls create mass ties, so report C with an IPCW variant alongside tie-aware Kendall (this last point is the notes' reasoning, not sourced).

## Rank-based incentive theory already covers the lemma's content

The notes' honest assessment is that the lemma is a consequence of composing a monotone rank map with a payoff schedule, and that the economic content has precedent. Classic contest theory assumes prizes are non-increasing in rank and studies effort, not the converse: Lazear and Rosen (J. Political Economy 89(5): 841-864, 1981, DOI 10.1086/261010) and Moldovanu and Sela (American Economic Review 91(3): 542-558, 2001, DOI 10.1257/aer.91.3.542) ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w0401), [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:91:y:2001:i:3:p:542-558); ABS). Fang, Noe and Strack (J. Political Economy 128(5): 1940-1975, 2020) show unequal prizes and more competition discourage effort with homogeneous contestants ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-705670.html); ABS). Dechenaux, Kovenock and Sheremeta survey the experimental contest literature (Experimental Economics 18(4): 609-669, 2015) ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:kap:expeco:v:18:y:2015:i:4:p:609-669); ABS).

The sports literature is the closest precedent for payoff inversion. Taylor and Trogdon (J. Labor Economics 20(1): 23-41, 2002) show NBA teams lose more when draft incentives reward it ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jlabec/v20y2002i1p23-41.html); ABS). Dagaev and Sonin (J. Sports Economics 19(8): 1122-1146, 2018) show that round-robin-plus-knockout systems with non-cumulative prizes are generically incentive incompatible ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:19:y:2018:i:8:p:1122-1146); ABS). Csato's papers give strategy-proofness conditions for qualification formats (arXiv 1712.04183, 1804.04422, 2104.03067; SNIP, and the notes warn the IDs are unmatched to titles). Fornwagner (J. Economic Psychology 75(B), 2019) finds NHL teams lose more once eliminated ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joepsy/v75y2019ipbs0167487017306001.html); ABS; page numbers not verified).

The tax-notch literature supplies the dominated-region analogy. Saez (AEJ: Economic Policy 2(3): 180-212, 2010, DOI 10.1257/pol.2.3.180) finds bunching at the first kink only ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aejpol:v:2:y:2010:i:3:p:180-212); ABS). Kleven and Waseem (QJE 128(2): 669-723, 2013, DOI 10.1093/qje/qjt004) show notches create dominated ranges and large bunching, yet many taxpayers in the dominated range do not respond, which implies frictions ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v128y2013i2p669-723.html); ABS). Kleven's "Bunching" survey is Annual Review of Economics 8: 435-464 (2016) ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/anr/reveco/v8y2016p435-464.html); ABS). Mapping Traze's rank 3 to rank 4 payoff drop (strategic-underperformance gain of 100, ten times the burn, per the notes) onto a notch is the notes' inference. The notch literature suggests a caveat: noise and inattention can blunt the response, so a strict "agents will tank" prediction should be tested, not assumed. For the linear-tax-plus-equal-rebate claim, no source was verified; the notes' derivation gives marginal return (1 - t) + t/n, positive for t < 1, so the effect is dilution, not reversal. Standard references (Sheshinski 1972; Mirrlees 1971; Holmstrom 1982) are **MEM only** and unverified. Altman and Tennenholtz (JAIR 2008) on ranking-system axioms is SNIP only ([PDF](https://moshet.net.technion.ac.il/files/2022/08/axiomatic-foundations-ranking-systems.pdf)). Culling versus gradual removal in evolutionary models (truncation selection, Baker 1985) was **not researched**.

## Cost metering: token charging has support, time charging has a cautionary precedent

Kapoor et al. ("AI Agents That Matter", arXiv 2407.01502) argue for reporting cost with accuracy, note that dollar prices change "overnight", and recommend reporting input and output token counts so cost can be recomputed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502); FULL for about 100k of 144k characters). This supports a token unit. The notes found no paper arguing wall-clock should be the primary cost. USACOArena is the only benchmark found that charges wall-clock (alpha x T, normalised API-price spend, hints at 500 to 1,500 credits), and it sets alpha = 0 in its main cross-model results because commercial APIs introduce "unfair network latency" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10182); FULL; the swarm-experiment alpha and appendices were not read). The notes found no rigorous study of wall-clock variance across hardware; the only evidence is vendor blogs (queueing, GPU SKU, batching), which are weak and flagged UNVERIFIED ([General Compute](https://www.generalcompute.com/blog/llm-token-generation-speed-how-providers-compare-in-2025?agent=true), [GMI Cloud](https://www.gmicloud.ai/blog/lowest-latency-ai-inference-provider-for-open-source-llms-2026-engineering-guide)). So "wall-clock is hardware- and load-dependent" is plausible and USACOArena's authors act on it, but the paper should say so as an engineering rationale, not a measured result, unless Traze runs its own variance test.

Hidden reasoning is billed at the output rate by the major providers, but visible text does not match billed counts. OpenAI reports `output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens` inside `output_tokens`, and `max_output_tokens` covers reasoning plus output, so a cap can end a call with no visible output while still billing ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning); FULL). Anthropic bills full thinking tokens even when only a summary is returned, and thinking counts toward `max_tokens` ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/thinking); FULL). Gemini thinking-token reporting is known only from forum threads, including a user claim of an 8.5x underreport on one model ([thread](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/bug-gemini-2-5-flash-lite-candidatestokencount-underreports-output-tokens-by-8-5x-vs-actual-billing/124448); anecdotal). DeepSeek-style hosts return `reasoning_content` separately ([Together](https://docs.together.ai/docs/inference/chat/reasoning); FULL, host docs not DeepSeek's own). vLLM and Ollama return reasoning in separate fields, and whether Ollama's `eval_count` includes thinking was **not verified** ([vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/reasoning_outputs/), [Ollama](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/thinking)). Epoch's Vending-Bench 2 review gives the strongest warning: its harness counted encrypted reasoning as ordinary text with tiktoken, so about 54,000 counted tokens in one median window held only about 4,000 real reasoning tokens, leaving unequal usable context across providers ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review); FULL). The lesson is to charge the API-reported usage, not a re-tokenisation of visible text.

Tokenizer dependence is well documented. Petrov et al. (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2305.15425) show up to 15x tokenized-length differences across languages ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425); ABS). A TensorZero blog (Apr 2026) reports up to 2.65x token differences between vendors on identical inputs, mostly on tool definitions ([blog](https://www.tensorzero.com/blog/stop-comparing-price-per-million-tokens-the-hidden-llm-api-costs/); secondary). No precedent was found for a character-based charge rule, so that is Traze's own design. Truncation is another documented distortion: "Who Thinks Best Depends on How Long You Let Them" (arXiv 2608.12150; SNIP) reports Qwen-3 32B truncated on 59.7% of GPQA items at a 4,096 budget versus 0.5% for LLaMA-3.3 70B ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12150)), and "Broken Chains" (arXiv 2602.14444; SNIP) reports truncated reasoning can be worse than none ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14444v1)). Traze's own observation (7 of 15 long generations hitting the cap, 1.9 to 2.7 chars per token, both from the notes and not independently checkable here) fits this literature. No source was found reporting within-model chars-per-token ranges, so treat that as Traze's measurement.

## How to word the novelty claims

Do not claim that Traze is the first to make LLM agents pay for their own compute or to use survival pressure; cite The Energy Society and USACOArena as precedent and position Traze against them. Do not claim the rank-schedule lemma as a theorem of independent interest; call it an elementary observation, cite Kleven and Waseem (dominated range) and Dagaev and Sonin / Csato (incentive incompatibility) as precedent, and state the result as an instance applied to an explicit rank-based tax/subsidy/cull schedule. Do not claim the one-shot versus gradual intuition is new; it follows from bandit and noisy-sorting theory (successive halving, Braverman-Mossel). What the notes support claiming, always with "to our knowledge", is an empirical comparison of removal rule and transfer schedule on how well survival order recovers planted skill, with censoring-aware metrics. Frame the token-versus-time metering choice as consistent with Kapoor et al. and with USACOArena's alpha = 0, and describe hardware dependence as a rationale unless measured. Frame hidden-reasoning charging as a consequence of provider billing and Epoch's tiktoken finding, not as an invention. Describe audit probes as new only after a targeted search for audit or honeypot mechanisms in agent benchmarks, which the notes never covered (CoffeeBench's prompt-level audit warning is the only related item). Mention that Agent Island's peer-vote bias and CoffeeBench's target-conditioned fraud motivate non-vote, non-target-gameable rules. In a short IEEE paper, one sentence per cluster plus the table above should suffice, and every FULL, ABS or SNIP flag should be resolved by opening the source before submission.

## Conclusion

The literature has built the ingredients separately: token-priced survival (Energy Society), compute-billed arenas (USACOArena), Bayesian rankings of elimination games (Agent Island), incentive-incompatibility theory, and provider reasoning-token billing. What it lacks, within the limits of a shallow search, is a controlled test of whether the elimination rule and rank schedule change how faithfully survival order reflects capability. That is the one position the notes support defending, and it is a validity claim, not a mechanism or metering claim.

The main risk to the paper is a reviewer pointing to Energy Society or USACOArena, so the related-work section should concede that overlap early. A secondary risk is that the planted-skill premise cannot be validated against real model skill, which no prior work does either; say so explicitly.
